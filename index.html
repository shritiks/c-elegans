Gene expression simulation in C. elegans

Four genes that handle inhibitory, excitatory and olfactory signalling in the nematode, and a simulated worm whose behaviour changes as you turn each one up or down.

Caenorhabditis elegans has 302 neurons and a behavioural repertoire simple enough to score by eye: crawl, turn, approach, avoid. That makes it a good animal for asking how the amount of one gene product changes what the whole animal does.

The model below reduces that question to four genes and three stimuli. Set expression with the sliders, put stimuli on the plate, and watch where the worm goes.

Three things to try. Put food and diacetyl on the plate, then : the worm leaves the odour for the lawn. and it stops responding to either. and it still finds its target, slowly.

The four genes

Each slider stands for one gene. Three act at the synapse: two load neurotransmitter into vesicles and one receives it. The fourth acts at the tip of a sensory neuron, where an odour is first detected.

Gene Product Where it acts Without it In this model
unc-47Chromosome III Vesicular GABA transporter. Loads GABA into synaptic vesicles. The 26 GABA neurons, including motor neurons that relax body-wall muscle on the far side of each bend. Both sides of the body contract at once when the animal is touched, and crawling is uncoordinated. Inhibitory drive. When it exceeds both eat-4 and odr-10, the worm ignores stimuli and wanders.
eat-4Chromosome III Vesicular glutamate transporter. Loads glutamate into synaptic vesicles. Glutamate-releasing neurons, among them many sensory neurons and the pharyngeal neuron M3. Pharyngeal pumping is abnormal and responses to touch and chemical cues are weakened. Weight given to food. With food and diacetyl both present, the worm goes to food when eat-4 exceeds odr-10.
odr-10Chromosome X Seven-transmembrane odorant receptor for diacetyl. Sensory cilia of the AWA olfactory neurons. Chemotaxis to diacetyl fails. Responses to other AWA-sensed odours remain. Weight given to the diacetyl source.
glr-1Chromosome III AMPA-type ionotropic glutamate receptor subunit. Command interneurons that set forward and backward movement. The animal does not back away from a touch to the nose. Crawling speed, and how often the worm changes heading while exploring.

The genes sit at different points on the path from stimulus to movement. ODR-10 detects an odour. Glutamate release, which depends on eat-4, carries signals from many sensory neurons onward. GLR-1 receives glutamate on the interneurons that decide direction. GABA release, which depends on unc-47, shapes the motor pattern that results. Behaviour reflects the balance between these excitatory and inhibitory signals, and that balance is what the sliders move.

The animal

An adult hermaphrodite is about 1 mm long and transparent, so its organs can be seen in a light micrograph of the living animal. Point at a region of the body, or choose an organ from the list.

What the model does, and what it leaves out

The worm follows four rules.

  • If unc-47 is higher than both eat-4 and odr-10, inhibition dominates. The worm ignores attractants and wanders, changing heading often.
  • Otherwise it heads for diacetyl or food. With both on the plate it chooses food if eat-4 exceeds odr-10, and diacetyl if not.
  • glr-1 sets crawling speed and how often the worm picks a new heading while exploring.
  • Within a fixed distance of the repellent, the worm turns away from it.

These rules are a sketch of the biology, and several things are missing.

  • There are no neurons or synapses. Expression levels are compared as numbers, and the larger one wins.
  • The worm steers straight at a source. A real worm cannot see where an odour comes from. It climbs the concentration gradient by turning more often when things get worse (Pierce-Shimomura et al., 1999) and by curving gradually towards higher concentrations (Iino and Yoshida, 2009).
  • Overexpressing unc-47 in a living animal would not make it ignore food. The first rule stands in for inhibition outweighing excitation in general.
  • There is one animal. Chemotaxis is normally measured across a population, as an index from −1 to 1.
